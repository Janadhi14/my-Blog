## Who was the buddha?
most commonly to **Siddhartha Gautama** (also spelled **Gautama** or **Gotama**), a spiritual teacher and philosopher who lived in ancient India around the 5th century BCE. He is regarded as the founder of **Buddhism**


- **Birth and Early Life**: Siddhartha was born into a royal family in the Shakya clan (in the region that is now part of Nepal). Despite his father’s efforts to shield him from hardship, he encountered suffering and became determined to find a way to overcome it.
    
- **Quest for Enlightenment**: Leaving his privileged life, he became an ascetic, practicing extreme austerities under various teachers. Ultimately, he concluded that strict asceticism did not lead to ultimate insight.
 
- He adopted the **Middle Way**—a balanced approach—and meditated deeply, culminating in his attainment of **enlightenment** (bodhi) under the Bodhi Tree in Bodh Gaya.
    
- **Teachings**: After enlightenment, Siddhartha was called “Buddha,” meaning “Awakened One.” He spent the rest of his life traveling and teaching what came to be known as the **Dharma** (the Buddha’s teachings). Key elements of his message include:
    
    - The **Four Noble Truths**, which analyze the nature of suffering and outline the path to freedom from suffering.
    - The **Noble Eightfold Path**, a practical guideline for ethical living, mental discipline, and wisdom.
- **Influence and Legacy**: His teachings spread gradually, evolving into a worldwide religion and philosophy with various traditions (Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana, among others). Buddhists revere him not as a deity but as a fully enlightened human being who shared a path that others could follow to achieve liberation from the cycle of suffering (samsara).
