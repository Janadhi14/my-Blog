## Encryption
- Hashing vs encryption
	- Encryption is a 2 way function
	- hashing is a one way function that scrambles a plain tedxt to produce a unique message 
2 types of encryption
Symmetric encryption - single shared key 
- AES 256bit key the go to encryption standard 
Asymmetric encryption
- public/public key encryption
	- RSA and ECC
- Anyone with the public key can encrypt a message but only the holder of the private key can decrypt the message 
- public keys are shared among communicating parties 
- private keys are kept secret 
Data:
-  to encrypt the message you will use the public key 
- to decrypt the receiver will use their private key


## Digital signatures 
- this uses asymmetric cryptography and to sign a message you use your own private key and to validate the signature you use a senders public key.
- A mathematical algo routinely used to  authenticity and integrity of a message
- message coudl mean an email, a credit card, transction ora digital document 
- create a virtual fingerprint that is unique to a person or entity 
- relies on asymmetric (public key ) cryptography and hash functions

## Hash function requirements
Good hash functions have 5 requirements:
- they must allow input of any length
- provide fixed length output 
- make it relatively easy to compute the hash for any input
- Provide **one-way** functionality (cannot be reversed)
- must be collision free (MD5 and SHA-2) - hash collisions. (2 inputs will never produce the same output)

## Cryptographic salt
Attackers may use rainbow tables - a precomputed values to identify commonly used passwords - commonly used password hashes 

Salt is random data that is added so the hash will look different 

Adding salts to the passwords functions to increase security as it minimises the effectiveness  of rainbow table attacks

## GRC 
GRC focuses on policies, popearional processes and technolgies to help the organisation reduce risk and improve compliance 
### Governance 
The system of rules, practices and processes and organisation uses to direct and control its activities 
- can be internal or external standards eg ISO45000

many governance activities arise from external standards, obligations and expectations 
- this creates a framework for oversight 
### Risk
- this is the process of identifying assessing and responding to threats events that can impact company or customer objectives 
- organisations face obvious external risk such as hackers and disasters but also internal risks 
- internal risks include leaks of sensitive data, intellectual property theft and fraud

### Compliance 
Adherence to regulations, whether multi-national, national, state or federal laws or industry that an organisation must follow . 
- Three tenants of compliance include:
	- data residency 
	- data sovereignty
	- data privacy 
#### Residency 
- governance of the physical locations of dtaa; where it can be stored and how and when it can be transferred, processes, or accesses internationally  
- These regulations can differ significantly depending on jurisdiction 
- Sovereignty- data is subject to the laws and regulations of the country/region in which its physically collected, held or processes 
	- particularly important with **personal data**(PII) which is regulated by multiple nations 
- GDPR -  applies to any company that has customers in that country, so if you operate in multiple countries then you are going to have to comply with the GDPR requirements
- Privacy - providing notice about the collection, processing, use and sharing of personal data are fundamental principles of privacy laws and regulations 
Personal data means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person
