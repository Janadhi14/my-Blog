## Detailed Reasons to Conduct a Wi-Fi Survey
Wi-Fi site surveys are indispensable processes for ensuring the optimal performance, coverage, and reliability of wireless networks. They are conducted for various critical reasons throughout the lifecycle of a WLAN:

*   **Design Validation (Pre-deployment Survey):** This type of survey, often referred to as a predictive or pre-deployment survey, is performed before any access points are physically installed. Its primary purpose is to:
    *   **Validate a Wireless LAN design with APoS survey:** By simulating the placement of access points (Access Point on a Stick - APoS) and taking measurements, designers can verify that their theoretical design will meet the required coverage, capacity, and performance objectives in the actual environment. This helps in identifying the best locations for access points and optimizing their configuration before full deployment.
    *   **Optimize AP Placement:** Determine the ideal number and placement of access points to achieve desired signal strength, coverage, and capacity, while minimizing interference.
    *   **Identify Potential Issues:** Uncover potential RF interference sources, building materials that attenuate signals, and other environmental factors that could impact network performance.

*   **Deployment Validation (Post-deployment Survey):** Conducted after the wireless network has been physically installed and configured, this survey is crucial for:
    *   **Validate an installed wireless network matches the design and meet the requirements:** This involves taking actual measurements of signal strength, noise, interference, and data rates across the entire coverage area. It confirms that the network is performing as designed and meets all specified requirements (e.g., minimum signal strength in certain areas, required throughput for applications).
    *   **Troubleshooting and Optimization:** Identify and address any coverage gaps, weak signal areas, or performance bottlenecks that may have emerged during or after installation.
    *   **Documentation:** Provide comprehensive documentation of the network's actual RF performance, which is invaluable for future troubleshooting, capacity planning, and network upgrades.

*   **Assessment Survey (Troubleshooting/Optimization Survey):** This type of survey is performed on an existing wireless network to:
    *   **Assess an existing wireless network against a set of requirements:** Evaluate the current state of the network against new or existing performance requirements, security policies, or user complaints. This can help in diagnosing connectivity issues, identifying unauthorized devices, managing user capacity, and pinpointing sources of interference.
    *   **Identify Coverage Holes and Interference:** Locate areas with poor coverage or high interference that are impacting user experience.
    *   **Plan for Upgrades/Changes:** Provide data to support decisions for network upgrades, expansions, or reconfigurations.

## General Wi-Fi Survey Rules for Accuracy and Effectiveness
To ensure the accuracy and effectiveness of any Wi-Fi survey, adherence to certain general rules is essential:

1.  **Click Accurately:**
    *   **Layout on Building plans often inaccurate:** Building blueprints or floor plans may not always be perfectly to scale or reflect the current physical environment. Relying solely on them can lead to inaccurate survey data.
    *   **Using fixed building features such as pillars / windows to orient yourself:** Always use physical, fixed landmarks within the environment (e.g., pillars, doorways, windows, corners) to orient yourself and accurately mark your location on the survey map. This ensures that the collected data points are precisely mapped to their real-world coordinates.
    *   **Click where the Nomad (or survey device) is located:** The data collected by your survey device (like the Hamina Nomad) is specific to its exact location at the moment of the 


click. Therefore, it is crucial to click the survey tool at the precise physical location where the device is collecting data.

2.  **Click on both sides of what you care about:**
    *   **Attenuating objects:** Attenuating objects are materials or structures that absorb or block Wi-Fi signals, causing a reduction in signal strength. Common attenuating objects include thick walls (especially concrete or brick), metal structures, water (e.g., large aquariums, plumbing), and even dense human bodies. When surveying, it is vital to take measurements on both sides of such objects. This allows the survey software to accurately map the signal attenuation caused by these barriers, providing a more realistic representation of Wi-Fi coverage and helping to identify areas where additional access points might be needed or where signal penetration is a challenge. By capturing data before and after these attenuating objects, the survey provides a comprehensive understanding of the RF environment and its impact on wireless performance.
