## Detailed Design from Survey Data
Designing a Wi-Fi network based on actual survey data is a critical process that ensures the deployed network meets performance requirements and provides optimal user experience. This approach moves beyond theoretical predictions by incorporating real-world RF characteristics of the environment. The process typically involves several stages:

### Design from Survey Data Off-site
While the initial survey is conducted on-site, the subsequent design work can often be performed off-site using specialized software. This phase focuses on analyzing the collected data to refine the network design:

*   **On-site Design RF Modelling – Onsite Survey of Design:** This refers to the process where the data collected during an on-site survey (e.g., signal strength, noise, interference, wall attenuations) is used to build or refine an RF model of the environment. This model is a digital representation that accurately reflects how radio signals propagate within the specific building. It's a crucial step in translating raw survey data into a usable design framework.
*   **Workshop Based on Survey Final Design:** Once the RF model is established, the design team can work in a "workshop" environment (i.e., using the design software off-site) to finalize the network design. This involves:
    *   **Optimizing Access Point Placement:** Adjusting the number, location, and orientation of access points based on the survey data to achieve desired coverage, capacity, and performance goals.
    *   **Channel Planning:** Developing an optimal channel plan to minimize co-channel and adjacent-channel interference.
    *   **Power Settings:** Fine-tuning the transmit power of each access point to ensure proper cell sizing and roaming behavior.
    *   **Antenna Selection:** Choosing appropriate antenna types and patterns to direct signals effectively.
    This iterative process, driven by real survey data, leads to a highly optimized and reliable network design.

*   **Existing Network Documentation (Define) methods:** When designing from survey data for an existing network, it's essential to integrate the new design with existing network documentation. This involves updating or creating comprehensive records that detail:
    *   **Access Point Locations:** Precise physical locations of all deployed APs.
    *   **Configuration Details:** SSID names, security settings, VLAN assignments, channel assignments, and power levels for each AP.
    *   **Coverage Maps:** Heatmaps showing signal strength, SNR, and other RF metrics.
    *   **Interference Sources:** Documented sources of interference, both Wi-Fi and non-Wi-Fi.
    *   **Cabling and Infrastructure:** Details about network cabling, switch ports, and power infrastructure supporting the wireless network.
    This documentation is vital for ongoing network management, troubleshooting, and future upgrades. It ensures that any changes made based on the survey data are accurately reflected in the network's operational records.

By leveraging survey data, network designers can create highly accurate and performant wireless networks that are tailored to the unique characteristics of each environment, leading to improved user satisfaction and reduced operational issues.
