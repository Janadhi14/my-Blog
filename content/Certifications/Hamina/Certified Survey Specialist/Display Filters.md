# Display Filters

Display filters control the colors shown during a survey



## Detailed Explanation of Display Filters
Display filters are a fundamental feature in Wi-Fi site survey software, such as Hamina Onsite, that allow users to control how the collected survey data is visualized on the floor plan. They are essentially a set of rules and settings that determine the colors, thresholds, and types of information displayed in the form of heatmaps.

### How Display Filters Work
Display filters work by taking the raw data collected during a survey (e.g., signal strength, noise, interference) and translating it into a color-coded graphical representation. This allows for a quick and intuitive understanding of the wireless environment. Key aspects of display filters include:

*   **Controlling Colors:** The most apparent function of display filters is to control the colors shown on the heatmap. Each color corresponds to a specific range of values for the selected metric. For example, in a signal strength heatmap:
    *   **Green** might represent strong signal strength (e.g., -30 to -60 dBm).
    *   **Yellow** might represent acceptable signal strength (e.g., -60 to -70 dBm).
    *   **Red** might represent weak signal strength (e.g., below -70 dBm).
    These color schemes are often customizable, allowing users to set their own thresholds and color palettes to match their specific requirements or preferences.

*   **Selecting Metrics:** Display filters allow users to choose which metric they want to visualize. Common metrics include:
    *   **Signal Strength (RSSI):** Shows the received signal strength from access points.
    *   **Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR):** Displays the ratio of signal strength to background noise, which is a key indicator of signal quality.
    *   **Interference:** Visualizes the level of interference from other Wi-Fi networks or non-Wi-Fi sources.
    *   **Channel Overlap:** Shows areas where multiple access points are operating on the same or overlapping channels, which can cause co-channel interference.
    *   **Data Rate:** Displays the expected data rates in different areas based on signal quality.

*   **Setting Thresholds:** Users can set specific thresholds for each metric to define what is considered good, acceptable, or poor performance. For example, a user might set a requirement that all areas must have a signal strength of at least -65 dBm. The display filter can then be configured to highlight any areas that do not meet this requirement.

*   **Filtering by SSID or AP:** Display filters can often be used to isolate and view data for specific SSIDs or individual access points. This is useful for analyzing the coverage of a particular network or troubleshooting issues with a specific AP.

### Importance of Display Filters
Display filters are crucial for making sense of the vast amount of data collected during a site survey. They provide a clear and concise visual representation of the wireless environment, enabling network engineers and administrators to:

*   **Quickly identify problem areas:** Easily spot areas with weak signal, high interference, or poor coverage.
*   **Validate network designs:** Compare the actual performance of the network against the design requirements.
*   **Troubleshoot connectivity issues:** Pinpoint the root cause of user complaints by visualizing the RF environment in their specific location.
*   **Communicate findings effectively:** Create clear and easy-to-understand reports and visualizations for stakeholders who may not be experts in wireless technology.

In essence, display filters are the lens through which raw survey data is transformed into actionable insights, making them an indispensable tool for effective Wi-Fi network design, validation, and optimization.
