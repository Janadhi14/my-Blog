## What is Threat intelligence?
Threat intelligence is the collection, analysis, and dissemination of information about current and potential cybersecurity threats that could harm an organization. It's essentially actionable knowledge that helps security teams make informed decisions about protecting their systems and data.

One of the main:

**Data Sources**: Threat intelligence draws from various sources including malware samples, network traffic, security incidents, open source intelligence (OSINT), commercial feeds, government advisories, and threat actor communications.

**threat landscape** refers to the complete range of cybersecurity threats, vulnerabilities, and attack vectors that organizations and individuals face at any given time. It's essentially a comprehensive view of "what's out there" that could harm your digital assets, data, or operations.

#### Internal Intelligence Collection
The collection of internal information from networks or information systems that are possibly under the influence of an adversary yet may not actually be owned or controlled by the threat actor or actors
- Intelligence drives operations allowing the team to plan prepare and execute the operations
- Internal Intelligence utilises the current data in the organisation to narrow down the threats 
- Examples
	- Internal environment
	- Email Abuse Metrics 
	- Malware Rebuilding reports
	- Storyboards on Cyber incidents 
	- Trent reporting
- CTI can receive reporting from the incident reporting team as either:
	- Threat hunting reports 
	- Incident reports 

**Threat Hunting Driven by CTI**:
- **TTP-driven hunts**: Start from known behaviors/tactics.
- **IOC-driven hunts**: Use known Indicators of Compromise.
- **Analytics-driven hunts**: Apply analytics/ML to find anomalies.

#### External Intelligence Collection
Data collected from peer organizations, InfoSec community, and broader cyber threat landscape

Functions to:
- provides context for the internal intelligence collection and helps narrow down the search area
- Not raw “intelligence” for InfoSec teams, but contextual validation/comparison

Sources of External Intelligence:
- Research reports (malware, APT groups, cybercrime)
- Indicators of Compromise (IOCs)
- Trend data
- Disclosed breaches/incidents
- Vulnerabilities and exploit development
- Threat intelligence sharing groups
- Open-source or low-cost tools


The **Collection Management Framework** ensures CTI teams gather the right data, from the right sources, in the right way. Internal collection provides actionable intelligence for operations, while external collection supplies context and validation. Together, they drive informed, intelligence-led security operations.
